# Wick

Wick is a local crypto strategy desk. It replays classical rules on Bitcoin, Ether, and the other USDT markets, fills each signal on the next bar’s open, and lines each run up against buy and hold.

The desk holds up to four slots. Each slot keeps its own symbol, spot or perpetual market, bar size, and range. A slot can also pin two more symbols beside the active one, three at most. Each pinned symbol keeps its own book socket, its own last price, and its own working ticket. Changing which of those three is active does not close the other sockets. The header follows the active slot and reads Binance spot or Kraken perp. A spot slot is long or flat. A perpetual slot can short, sets leverage, and applies funding. Perp candles, funding, the contract list, and the live book are Kraken USD perpetuals. Spot stays on Binance. A perp slot does not plot a spot kline. A pinned socket stays open until that symbol leaves the pin set or the slot is closed.

Costs are part of the test. Fees and slippage are charged on the way in and the way out. The base balance starts at $10,000 and can be changed per run. Risk per trade is the percent of equity lost if the stop is hit. It starts at 1% and cannot be set above 5%. The position is that amount divided by the stop distance, and the stop is the 14-bar average true range at the signal. Fee and slippage are inputs, charged on the entry and the exit. A spot slot stays long or flat, and its trade log does not list shorts. A perpetual slot takes a short when the rule has a short side. Heikin-ashi, break of structure, and Renko stay long or flat. Buy and hold stays long. On perpetuals a short is a short position, not a borrow. Setup sets leverage from 1× to 20×, default 3×, only on a perpetual slot. Open perpetual positions pay or receive the public funding rate. If price trades through the liquidation price, from leverage and a 0.40% maintenance margin, the trade closes there and is marked liquidated. A failed funding request does not invent a zero rate.

## Run the API

```bash
cd crypto-backtester/backend
python -m venv .venv
source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
uvicorn app.main:app --reload --port 8000
```

Spot USDT pairs load candles from Binance spot klines. Perpetual candles, funding, and the contract list come from the matching Kraken USD perpetual (Bitcoin is PF_XBTUSD), including the relative funding rate. A failed Kraken request keeps the last good series and marks it stale. It does not invent a funding rate or a candle. Missing bars stay missing: the previous close is not copied into the hole, and the header counts the gaps. Candles are UTC. Other dollar symbols still read Coinbase and fall back to Kraken spot. The desk asks for 1-minute, 3-minute, 5-minute, 15-minute, 30-minute, 1-hour, 4-hour, and daily bars. Binance already publishes those sizes. On Coinbase, three-minute bars are bundled from 1-minute candles, thirty-minute bars from 5-minute candles, and four-hour bars from hourly candles. The first request for a market downloads history and caches it under `backend/data/cache`. Live updates ask for the newest bars and skip that cache. A short bar size only returns as far back as the exchange will page, and the status line says so.

## What a run reports

- Equity against a buy-and-hold benchmark that pays the same fee and slippage
- Drawdown, CAGR, Sharpe, and Sortino (365-day crypto year)
- Win rate, expectancy, and profit factor on the same closed round trips. Profit factor is gross winning return divided by gross losing return. An open trade is marked in the log and left out. A run whose closed returns are −13.8%, −2.6%, and +0.8% has a profit factor below 1
- The trades themselves, including a position that is still open at the end of the window
- Expectancy on closed trades, average win times win rate minus average loss times loss rate, and how many bars the worst drawdown takes to reclaim its peak
- The same pass split into the first 70% of bars and the last 30%. Return, max drawdown, Sharpe, and trade count sit side by side. Parameters are not fit again on the holdout. The comparison table can show the full run, the in-sample window, or the holdout.
- Three walk-forward windows on that same pass, plus the in-sample and holdout slices. Windows, beside Desk and Journal, lists In-sample, Holdout, Window 1, Window 2, and Window 3: return, max drawdown, Sharpe, and trades. A row with no closed trade says so in the trades cell and does not show that return as a finished trade. A missing slice stays a dash. Nothing is optimized again. Comparison stays the run table. Its Full, In-sample, and Holdout buttons only change which slice that table shows. Nudge, in Setup, reruns a rule that has a fast length at fast−1, fast, and fast+1, and those three returns sit at the top of Comparison. They are not drawn on the equity chart. If holdout Sharpe is negative while the full-sample Sharpe is positive, the run says so.
- MC 5% equity on the run summary: the 5th percentile ending equity after 500 redraws of the closed-trade returns, compounded from the starting balance. The closed-trade count sits beside the figure. Fewer than ten closed trades is marked thin sample. The same thin-sample line appears on Windows, Cross, and Diff when the kept or active run has fewer than ten closed trades, so a short sample is not read as settled.

A perpetual slot shows the last Kraken funding rate already on the candle tape, with that rate’s time in UTC. If the series is missing and stale, the line says funding didn't load. Spot slots hide it. The run summary shows funding paid only on a perp.

A slot can arm a price above, a price below, or a 60-second delta. Setup also holds a 60-second delta for that slot. Next to Arm, the desk shows the price or the 60-second threshold it is watching, and idle until the last trade or that delta crosses it. The label then reads fired and names the side: above, below, buy, or sell. Clicking a book ladder price sets that Arm level (above the mid, or below it) and shows the level next to Arm. When Arm fires it also asks for a browser Notification with the symbol, side, and price. Settings has an Arm sound toggle, off by default; when on, Arm plays a short beep on fire. Stand-down still blocks Arm. If the book goes quiet for 15 seconds and the last candle poll is older than the bar’s poll window, the slot badges stale and Arm stays blocked until a fresh tick. A slot that has not heard a tick yet does not badge stale while it is still connecting. After the tab idles, coming back reconnects the books and retries the candle poll so a short freeze does not leave a stale wall. Flatten, beside Arm, closes the open desk position at the mark, disarms Arm, and writes the close to the journal. Stand-down is unchanged. The journal lists that alert under the trade table. It does not place an order. The same row sets the risk stamp for the desk: DEFENSIVE, NORMAL, or PRESS. NORMAL is the default. That stamp is stored on each journal row and each working ticket. The header has a session light: open, closed, or stand-down. Saturday and Sunday start on stand-down until you set the light. Arm does not take a level, and an armed level does not fire, while the light is stand-down. Setup also has max bars open (blank by default). An open trade past that count gets a time badge beside the slot and on the journal row. Close on time stop stays off unless you turn it on. Setup also has an optional bias timeframe, strictly higher than the slot’s bar size. When set, the rule may only go long while that tape’s trend agrees (close above its SMA). The slot shows a with trend or against badge.

The cost line under the metric strip reads before fees, fees paid, slippage paid, and after costs. Fee and slippage sit together in Setup, and both are charged on the entry and the exit. The metric strip also shows risk of ruin: the chance of a 20% drawdown from win rate and risk per trade. Under ten closed trades it stays a thin sample dash.

Keep this run stores a strategy, a run, and one trade row per fill, then opens Saved and copies a share URL with symbol, market, timeframe, rule, and params. Opening that URL restores the slot without an account. The run keeps its range, metrics, note, the in-sample slice, the holdout slice, three walk-forward windows, and the fee line: before fees, fees, slippage, and after costs. It points at those trades by id and does not keep the candle history. Those fills stay on the run. They are not added to the trade journal. Export JSON, beside that button, downloads the stored strategy, run, and linked trades. If this run has not been kept, the file is the active run and is not written into the book. Export trades CSV on the desk reads the linked trades, or the trades on screen when the run has not been kept. Saved, under Pages, lists kept runs: strategy name, symbol, market, return, note, and date kept. Each row can Lock; a locked run’s params cannot be edited in place, and changing them on the desk duplicates the run. Filters for symbol and strategy. 50 rows, newest first, with a count. Opening a row restores the slot from the strategy and run ids. Candle history is not copied into Saved. Working, on the journal, holds only live tickets: open, armed, or not yet kept. A kept run is removed from Working. A ticket has a symbol, a side, a strategy, an entry, a stop, a size, and R, plus a scale-out at an R multiple (default 2R) and a percent of size to close there (default 50). The desk’s risk stamp and the session light at save time sit on the ticket too. Filters for symbol, strategy, side, risk, and session are fields on the ticket. The list is 50 rows, newest first, with the match count. When the last trade on a pinned symbol trades through the entry, that ticket writes one journal row. The entry on the row is the fill price. Stop and R stay. The exit stays empty until the trade closes. When mark hits the scale-out target, the journal closes that slice as a partial and leaves the runner open, linked by id. Promote, on the ticket, marks it armed, copies the symbol, side, entry, stop, size, and R onto that slot’s Arm line, and writes one note on the journal row so the ticket and the row share an id.

Account, beside the Wick name, reads Account · local until you sign in. That local book stays in this browser. Create or sign in with a name and password. The server stores a hash of the password and one user id. Every browser that signs in with that name gets the same user id, and the button then shows the name. Saved runs and journal trades are stored on that user. A saved run is the strategy, symbol, market, params, metrics, note, and its trades. It does not keep candle history. On sign-in this browser loads that user's Saved and Journal. Keep this run and a logged trade write to the server. Another browser signed in as the same user sees them after refresh. Export JSON, beside Keep this run, remains a backup download. The account also stores slots, favourites, and pane sizes. The desk allows up to about 8MB; a push that still overflows keeps open trades and the newest closed ones so sync does not stall. Create starts from the desk already on this browser. Sign in replaces this browser with the account on this API. Sign out leaves a copy here and stops sending changes.

Settings, beside the Wick name, mints a bot key. A Grok bot sends that key to change the open desk: market, bar size, range, rule, combined strategies, and risk. The header is Wick, Pages, Spot or Perp, symbol, and timeframe. Desk is the default page; click Wick to return to it. ⌘K or Ctrl+K opens a command palette for Pages, symbol search, and Keep this run. It is not a new page. Pages holds Journal, Saved, Windows, Replay, Paper, Cross, Correlation, and Diff. Paper lists paper return, backtest return, and the difference for each kept run on the same rule and window. Cross-book and Correlation each keep an editable market list (up to twelve), stored with the workspace. Add any USDT market from the exchange list for that venue; defaults are BTC/ETH/SOL on Cross and BTC/ETH/SOL/XRP on Correlation. Pin puts a second slot beside the active one, each with its own book and backtest. The journal trade table defaults to open trades, then closed trades from the last 30 days: symbol, strategy, side, risk, from, to, open or closed, and an Archive filter for closed trades older than 30 days. It shows 50 rows a page, newest first, with the match count. Above the filtered totals, a week strip shows this UTC week’s net P&L, win rate, and average R from logged journal closes (not the backtest); an empty week stays a dash. A row stores ids and fields only — symbol, side, entry, exit, size, pnl, fee, slippage, strategy id, note, source — not candle history. The log follows the active slot. A Kraken perp slot is labeled KRAKEN PERP and writes market perp. A Binance spot slot is labeled BINANCE SPOT and writes market spot. Side, symbol, and tick size follow that market. A perp fill is not listed in the spot log. A backtest kept from the desk is not in that table. Searching does not pull every row into the page. Export JSON and Export trades CSV on the journal use those same filters, including Archive. Profit factor and expectancy on that page use the closed rows in the filter. A blank exit stays open and is left out. An open journal row and the book position for that symbol are the same trade. If the book is flat while the row is open, the row reads stale. If the book has a position and the journal has no open row for it, the slot reads unsynced. Close on the row, Flat on the slot, or Flatten beside Arm, closes both at the last price; Flatten also clears the armed level. The same key can log one trade with log-trade: symbol, side, price, size, and an optional exit, strategy, and note. That writes one trade row. It does not append the trade to a JSON file. The key does not rewrite the app, and Wick stores only a hash. The example request is in that settings sheet.

Each slot keeps up to five runs in the browser. A run is a name, a rule, its parameters, a fee, a base balance, and a risk per trade. The same run can combine every other strategy on the list. A rule already on the run is not offered again. A position opens only when the main rule and every combined strategy want the same side. The market belongs to the slot, so every run on that slot shares it. Spot is the Binance USDT spot book. Perp is the Kraken USD perpetual list. Bitcoin, Ether, Solana, XRP, and Dogecoin sit first, then the rest of the contracts. Search ranks an exact ticker, then a prefix, then a name. Picking a symbol in the header adds it to the active slot’s pin set until that set holds three. A fourth choice replaces only the active symbol and leaves the other two pinned. A favourite still opens a spot slot when there is room. The rule list is buy and hold, SMA crossover, EMA crossover, Donchian breakout, time-series momentum, Bollinger mean reversion, Bollinger breakout, RSI reversal, MACD trend with an ATR trail, Keltner breakout, Supertrend, opening-range breakout, previous-day high/low break, gap fade, gap continuation, VWAP reclaim, ATR chandelier, a dual moving average with a longer filter, fair value gap, candlestick trigger, Heikin-ashi trend, break of structure, change of character, supply and demand, RSI divergence, and Renko trend. Heikin-ashi labels the price chart Smoothed and draws the smoothed candles. Elliott Wave, harmonic patterns, Gann fans, moon phases, and hand-drawn trend lines are not on the list. Run and Run all belong to the active slot and calculate off the desk thread, so the book keeps repainting once a second. The comparison names the rule beside the run, and the equity legend does the same. It sorts by Sharpe, win rate, profit factor, CAGR, and trade count. A hidden run stays in the list and drops off the equity chart.

The workspace bar switches slots. Pin one slot beside the active one and both books stay on screen. From 1024px up the rail is a sidebar that starts at 200px. Drag its right edge to make it thinner, down to 112px, or wider up to 280px. That width is remembered with the book. Below 1024px the rail is a horizontal strip. It lists the open slots and favourites with the last price and the 24h change from the socket, on a spot slot and on a perp slot. A missing quote stays a dash. Clicking a slot row focuses it. Clicking a favourite opens a spot slot.

The header keeps every bar size on screen, from 1m through 1D. The status line gives up space first, and the bar sizes wrap onto a second row only when the window is too narrow for one. The run summary is one compact row above the price chart. With one slot on screen and a mouse on a tall window, the chart is on the left and the order book runs beside the chart and the results under it, down to the bottom of that panel. On a touch screen, and in a short landscape window, the book stays only as tall as the price chart, so the ladder scrolls inside that height and the results use the full width underneath. Those results are one view at a time, behind Equity, Comparison, and Trades. Equity shows the dollar figure, the curve, and the drawdown. Pin a second slot and each chart keeps its own book at that chart’s height. Below 1024px the page itself scrolls, so the chart, the book, and the results can move up under the header. A wide window keeps the panes on one screen, and the equity curve fills the results pane. On a touch screen, including an iPad turned sideways, Setup is a button along the bottom that opens the runs. A mouse keeps that panel open on the right. The book stays beside the price chart, the same three tabs switch the results, and the candle plot keeps its height. Drag the vertical split to resize the book. Drag the horizontal split under the candles to scale Equity, Comparison, and Trades together; the candle plot takes the rest of that column. Both sizes are remembered in the browser. The book stays on the right of the price chart, including on a narrow screen. An open journal position on a slot shows open R from entry, stop, and mark beside the last price. A flat slot hides it. Give-back still flags when open profit falls under the setup share of the best open profit.

The top bar sets the active slot’s venue (Spot or Perp), market, and bar size (1m, 3m, 5m, 15m, 30m, 1h, 4h, 1D). The range (1 day, 5D, 1M, 3M, 1Y, 2Y, 4Y, 6Y, All) sits on the chart. The price axis fits the high and low of the candles on screen. The status line says Binance spot or Kraken perp for that slot. A gap count appears when bars are missing. Candles shows that slot’s last kline fetch. The book clock is separate and turns stale after three quiet seconds. A year is 366 days, so 2Y, 4Y, and 6Y ask for 732, 1464, and 2196 days. Hourly bars and coarser can cover those windows. Finer bars stay on the deepest tape that size can carry, and the status line says so. The price pane is candlesticks. Session shades weekend bars on that same plot. CVD draws the trade tape as a cumulative buy-minus-sell line on that same plot, and the control stays hidden while the tape is empty. The last candle, the price beside it, and the header price are the book’s last trade. If no trade has arrived, those three show the mid once, labeled MID with a space. Clicking a trade in the log windows the chart around that entry and shows the rule values that fired. Full tape clears that window. Enlarge is the control that opens that slot’s chart across the screen, with volume under the candles, drag-to-zoom, and a reset that fits the tape. The equity plot is dark, with the strategy in cyan and buy and hold dashed. A drawdown pane sits under it, and a log toggle scales the equity axis only.

Each open slot has its own book. Spot uses the public Binance spot stream: a 20-level depth snapshot, the trade tape, and the 24h ticker. Perps use the Kraken futures stream for that USD perpetual (Bitcoin is PF_XBTUSD): the book, trades, and ticker. The 24h change is the last price against the session open. Closing a slot is what unsubscribes it. Asks sit above the spread, bids below it, best prices nearest the middle, and the middle row shows the last trade, the spread, and the mid. The last cell stays Last. The mid is only the mid column. Prices follow the contract tick size. Size, the cumulative total, and each print keep four decimal places, including once the number passes one thousand, so a fractional step still changes the liquidity. Quote notional uses K / M / B, and the depth bars scale off notional. The ladder repaints once a second from the latest snapshot. If the socket goes quiet, the last good ladder stays up with a stale flag. Nothing is invented to fill a gap. A changed size flashes for a moment, new prints enter the 40-row tape, and a 60-second buy-minus-sell delta marks a one-row histogram. The forming candle on the chart takes those trades once a second. The book is not an input to Run or Run all.
